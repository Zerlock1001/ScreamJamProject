Trump ties $5,000 checks and other benefits to a Republican midterm win

At a rally in Greenville, North Carolina, the president said a new round of promises would reach Americans only if his party keeps Congress.

By the Harbor Ledger staff · Updated 9:40 a.m. ET

GREENVILLE, N.C. — President Donald Trump used a Tuesday rally at Pitt–Greenville Airport to tell supporters that a fresh set of federal benefits would depend on Republicans holding Congress in November.

The promise drawing the most notice is a payment of more than $5,000. On the road this week he has put the same condition on other offers. In Kansas, while campaigning for Senator Roger Marshall, he said summer limits on fuel blended with 15 percent ethanol would be lifted. In Kentucky he spoke of a $100 million investment in a pharmaceutical plant. In Ohio he said he would look “very strongly” at the state for a Space Force academy. Each, as he has framed it, waits on his preferred Republicans winning and on the party keeping control of Congress.

Democrats say the president is using the power of the office as a campaign reward, and as a warning. Doubt about the checks is wider than that argument. An NBC News Decision Desk poll found that 14 percent of U.S. adults expect to receive the $5,000 payment Trump has described. Among people who call themselves Republicans, 47 percent said they do not expect a check.

The Greenville stop, on October 20, was one piece of a late tour before the November 3 midterms. Trump has been asking voters to treat a Democratic Congress as the thing that would cancel what he has been calling “goodies” from the stage.

No bill, payment date, or agency plan for the checks has been published. Until that paperwork exists, the figure remains a pledge made on the trail, not a benefit people can apply for.

Also this week

On October 19 the Agriculture Department said it would make $8 billion available in one-time harvest payments to farmers, citing market disruptions and higher costs for fuel and other inputs.
